### -10 bounded persona-state adaptation - increment 1 `complete`; -10b see "Remaining executable work"
Delivered and deployed (PR #103; wording fix #108): closed style dimensions per persona (`response_depth`, `technical_depth`, `structure`, `intervention_style`) adapt through evidence -> candidate -> deterministic evaluation -> authority -> revision -> rollback. Structural guarantee against prompt self-modification: a revision can only select among fixed reviewed sentences, appended AFTER the untouched foundation prompt; no user text is ever stored in or rendered into a persona prompt. Authority as routing: explicit durable instruction promotes (household-wide unless it names a persona), feedback is a shadow-only correction (three consistent -> eligible awaiting ratification), one-off requests shape that turn only, reserved matters and `persist_turn=false` capture nothing; `/misumi/persona-state` API records the ratifying principal; `MISUMI_PERSONA_STATE=0` kill switch. Live-proven on a scratch instance with the real model (`evals/misumi-persona-state/2026-10-06-live-proof.md`): mean reply length about 66-70 words baseline vs 28 with a turn request vs 53-60 with the first durable wording (restart-persistent; restored after rollback with history kept); an eligible candidate stays inert until operator promotion (principal recorded), then effective, then rolled back; reserved matters not captured; an injected instruction captured only as style with nothing leaked. A direct experiment (`2026-10-06-style-placement-and-wording-experiment.md`) showed placement is irrelevant (~0.70 of baseline whether system, late or user) and WORDING decides the effect (shipped sentence 0.76, "at most two sentences" 0.47); the 0.47 wording was adopted (#108) and a stronger variant that told the persona to drop caveats was rejected on honesty/safety grounds. A second experiment (`2026-10-06-style-effect-experiment.md`) measured every fixed sentence against a mechanical metric: thorough 1.87x words, plain 0.65x long words, stepwise 100% numbered replies, brief 0.45x words worked; `proactive` produced NO next-step offers, `bullets` only 50% and `technical` only +19%, so those three were reworded and re-measured (offers 0% -> 67%, bullets 100%, technical +49%; #110); `prose` is the default value (it renders no sentence), and `reactive` is INCONCLUSIVE on provoking prompts (the shipped sentence moved offers 0.44 -> 0.67 in one run and 0.50 -> 0.25 in another; candidates 0.33 / 0.17; not changed - needs a larger N). Latent bug found by the live demo and fixed (#104): `_named_persona_in_prompt` substring-matched persona `l`'s one-letter display name.

### -11 dynamic multi-persona team formation - software `complete`; evaluation stage v2 `complete` (verdict recorded)
Delivered and deployed (PR #104, calibration #107): `src/misumi_team_formation.py` plans one lead plus at most two justified supports, deterministically, with a recorded reason for every outcome; Aoteru-as-lead behaviour unchanged; a specialist lead recruits only along its OWN ratified `consults` edges (contract rule 5), never Aoteru, never itself, never duplicate expertise, never on reserved matters; justification is `named` or `intent`+complex; no recruitment is first-class and a named persona outside the edges is reported as excluded. `/misumi/respond` returns a structured `team` block; a justified team routes domain requests through the model.
Earlier indicative benchmark (N=4, keyword checker; `evals/misumi-team/results/2026-10-06-team-benchmark.md`): team 13-14 of 20 planted conflicts vs solo 6-9, but supports flagged `RISK:` in 35 of 36 consults before the calibration in #107.
**Evaluation v2 (pre-registered, merged BEFORE data - `evals/misumi-team/eval-v2/PREREGISTRATION.md`):** 8 conflict/clean task pairs, N=12 per prompt per condition (384 replies), an independent-family primary judge (Nemotron via aoteru) plus a same-family secondary (qwen3:8b), false-alarm measurement on clean twins, a decision rule fixed in advance. **Result (2026-10-06, `evals/misumi-team/eval-v2/RESULTS.md`).** The pre-registered solo-vs-team comparison gave `SENSITIVITY GAINED AT THE PRICE OF FALSE ALARMS (not a net improvement)`, capped at indicative: the lead surfaced more planted problems (qwen3:8b judge 97% vs 89%; llama3.1:8b 44% vs 18%) but raised false alarms on clean requests (43% vs 0%; 82% vs 54%; AI audit 4/12 vs 1/12). Deviations disclosed: the pre-registered Nemotron judge was infeasible (cold-load 502 timeouts) and the llama3.1:8b substitute proved unreliable (58% agreement with a 48-row AI audit vs 94% for qwen3:8b); judge kappa 0.34. Two post-hoc fixes were measured on the same task set: summarising a support's `OK:` as "no issue found" removed false alarms but cut detection to 75% (-13.5 points vs solo, reassurance harm); dropping `OK:` contributions entirely (#116, `teamdrop`) gave detection 93% vs solo 89% (+4.2, CI -4.2 to +12.5) with false alarms 1% vs 0%, about one second more latency. **Final claim: `teamdrop` is a harm-reduction repair (it removes the false-alarm cost); it is NOT evidence that multi-persona teams improve answer quality - benefit remains unproven, and the detection gain is small, non-significant and NOT shown to help by the pre-registered bar.** `MISUMI_CONSULT=0` still disables teams.
Open: Sol value-vs-theatre judgement remains debt (D2) - and, per the user's 2026-10-06 instruction, -11 and -10 are the PRIORITY retrospective reviews when the lab/Sol lane returns.
